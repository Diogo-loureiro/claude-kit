# The TabNews post "Programando em Alto Nível na era da IA: É possível?" and Fabio Akita's 2025–2026 positions on AI coding, compared with Anthropic's agentic-coding guidance

> Research date: 2026-10-31. **Authorship correction first:** the TabNews post is **not by Fabio Akita**. It is a repost by TabNews user *JoasSurfer* of an article by **Sergio Oliveira Jr.** (soliveira.pages.dev). It does not mention Akita. Because the user attributed it to Akita, these notes cover both: (A) the actual post, and (B) Akita's own 2026 writing on akitaonrails.com, which is the likely intended source. Both are then compared with Anthropic.

## What exactly does the post argue (thesis, workflow, productivity, role of programmer, tests, specs, review, cost)?

### Takeaway
The post is a short (about 5 minutes to read) essay by Sergio Oliveira Jr., reposted on TabNews. It argues that AI is the next abstraction layer after binary, assembly and high-level languages. Natural language is ambiguous where code is formal, so expertise moves to asking the right questions and evaluating the output. Its advice is to keep the productivity gains while verifying with tests and examples, code review and domain experts. It gives no specific workflow, tools, numbers or cost analysis.

### Cited Findings
- **Authorship/provenance:** TabNews post by user "JoasSurfer", shown as "2 meses atrás" (no exact date). It names its original source as `https://soliveira.pages.dev/pt-br/blog/programar-um-nivel-acima/`. Fabio Akita is not mentioned anywhere — [TabNews post](https://www.tabnews.com.br/JoasSurfer/programando-em-alto-nivel-na-era-da-ia-e-possivel)
- **Original author/date:** Sergio Oliveira Jr., published 13 Sep 2026, 5-minute read. It credits Otavio Lemos's video "A IA vai acabar com a programação?" and does not mention Akita — [soliveira.pages.dev original](https://soliveira.pages.dev/pt-br/blog/programar-um-nivel-acima/)
- **Thesis (abstraction ladder):** "Agora podemos descrever uma tarefa em linguagem natural e pedir a um modelo de IA que produza uma implementação." / "O efeito prático é familiar: conseguimos dedicar mais atenção ao problema que queremos resolver." — [TabNews post](https://www.tabnews.com.br/JoasSurfer/programando-em-alto-nivel-na-era-da-ia-e-possivel)
- **Historical resistance:** The post cites Betty Holberton's 1974 memo on the 1957 Fortran tests and John Backus's history of Fortran. Its argument is that compilers earned trust through engineering, so skepticism about a new abstraction layer is not new — [TabNews post](https://www.tabnews.com.br/JoasSurfer/programando-em-alto-nivel-na-era-da-ia-e-possivel)
- **Core caveat (non-formality):** "Uma linguagem de programação tem regras formais; um pedido em linguagem natural pode deixar decisões importantes em aberto." The same prompt can yield different implementations — [TabNews post](https://www.tabnews.com.br/JoasSurfer/programando-em-alto-nivel-na-era-da-ia-e-possivel)
- **Worked example:** "Calcule o total dos pagamentos aprovados." → "Um pagamento aprovado, mas ainda não liquidado, deve fazer parte do total?" (it also raises currency and refunds) → "Uma implementação curta pode esconder um problema de negócio difícil." — [TabNews post](https://www.tabnews.com.br/JoasSurfer/programando-em-alto-nivel-na-era-da-ia-e-possivel)
- **Recommendations (verification):** "Podemos esclarecer essas regras no pedido e pedir testes com exemplos concretos." / "Também podemos examinar o código, consultar quem conhece o processo e verificar o comportamento do sistema." — [original](https://soliveira.pages.dev/pt-br/blog/programar-um-nivel-acima/)
- **Role of programmer:** "A experiência aparece na escolha dessas perguntas e na avaliação das respostas." Knowledge of lower levels (memory, databases, concurrency) is still needed to diagnose problems — [TabNews post](https://www.tabnews.com.br/JoasSurfer/programando-em-alto-nivel-na-era-da-ia-e-possivel)
- **Productivity/conclusion:** It acknowledges "ganhos absurdos de produtividade" without quantifying them. "Levantar da mesa, abrindo mão dos ganhos de produtividade, seria um erro." / "Com a IA, estamos renegociando o tamanho dessa parte e os critérios para confiar nela." — [TabNews post](https://www.tabnews.com.br/JoasSurfer/programando-em-alto-nivel-na-era-da-ia-e-possivel)
- **Not covered:** costs, specific tools (no Claude Code or other agent named), CLAUDE.md, planning workflow, or metrics. The only code is a short Python snippet — [TabNews post](https://www.tabnews.com.br/JoasSurfer/programando-em-alto-nivel-na-era-da-ia-e-possivel)
- **Comments (2):** *realfakenerd* says the bigger problem is managers who expect complex projects to be done overnight. *cogumellum* argues AI differs from compilers because it infers implementations, so results vary and can hide decisions. It recommends small, well-defined, test-backed tasks, then gradually delegating core parts with human review and observability — [TabNews post](https://www.tabnews.com.br/JoasSurfer/programando-em-alto-nivel-na-era-da-ia-e-possivel)

### Inferences
- The post is a conceptual essay, not a workflow guide. Its only practical recommendations are clarify requirements, request example-based tests, review code, ask domain experts and observe behavior. All of them fit Anthropic's guidance, but they are generic.
- The "AI = next compiler" framing has a weak point that the post itself concedes: compilers are deterministic and formally specified, and LLMs are not. The commenter *cogumellum* makes this point more sharply.
- The user's attribution to Akita may come from overlapping themes. Akita also argues that "AI is a new compiler for an old discipline" (see below), but the two texts are independent.

### Gaps
- The exact TabNews publication date isn't on the page; the original is dated 13 Sep 2026.
- No bio was found for Sergio Oliveira Jr.

## What is Akita's broader, most recent position (2025–2026) on AI coding?

### Takeaway
Akita went from skeptic ("LLMs são loot boxes", May 2025) to heavy user. He spent a 500+ hour "vibe coding" marathon (Feb–May 2026) on 24 repos, mostly with Claude Code, which he prefers for its harness. His conclusion is that AI makes an experienced programmer about 5x faster (10x "com slop"), but only with XP discipline: TDD, CI, small releases, pair-programming-style steering and per-commit review. He rejects one-shot prompting, spec-driven development, multi-agent orchestration and "harness/loop engineering" as hype. By Oct 2026 he argues models have plateaued and are commodities ("competente o suficiente" when paired with an agent and tests), and that the engineering around them is what differentiates.

### Cited Findings
**Trajectory and volume**
- Blog index: "RANT - LLMs são LOOT BOXES!" (2025-05-02), "AGI ou Skynet não vão chegar tão cedo" (2025-06-18). Then a burst of 2026 posts: Claude Code vibe-coding projects (FrankMD, M.Akita Chronicles, Frank Sherlock, FrankMega), "RANT: o Akita abriu as pernas pra IA??" (2026-02-24), and many LLM benchmarks through Oct 2026 — [akitaonrails.com index](https://akitaonrails.com/)

**Marathon results (self-reported)**
- After roughly one month: 692 commits, 4,057 tests, 4 projects. Frank Sherlock had 17,210 lines of Rust and 10,863 of TypeScript in 7 days, with 7 releases in 7 days. M.Akita Chronicles had about 200 user stories in 8 days, which he compares to an estimated 10–15 weeks for a 2–3 developer Scrum team. FrankMega took 1 day versus an estimated 1–2 weeks. Post-launch work produced 125 commits across the 4 projects — [Software Nunca Está 'Pronto' (2026-03-01)](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/03/01/software-nunca-esta-pronto-4-projetos-a-vida-pos-deploy-e-por-que-one-shot-prompt-e-mito/)
- Final tally: 500+ hours (Feb–May 2026), almost half a million lines of code and docs, 24 repos. He rates them from 1 to 8 out of 10 and gives none a 10. ai-jail scored 8; FrankSherlock 2; FrankClaw, shadPS4 and easy-subtitle 1. Claimed gain is about 5x, "possivelmente 10x com slop junto." He calls the marathon a success for his question (can you code 100% with frontier LLMs: "dá"), but most projects were throwaway experiments — [Terminando Minha Maratona de IA (2026-05-14)](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/05/14/terminando-maratona-ia-sucesso-ou-fracasso/)
- Lessons: vibe coding always produces slop, so review commit by commit and PR by PR. "Quando você acha que 'terminou', na verdade é a versão 0.1." "Projeto sem usuário está morto." "IA não substitui um bom programador. IA reflete e acelera quem você é." "Não pague por token" (use subscription plans). If the goal is a business, take at least 10x more care — [Terminando Minha Maratona](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/05/14/terminando-maratona-ia-sucesso-ou-fracasso/)

**Workflow and recommendations**
- **Human decides, agent types:** "O agente escreve o código. Eu decido qual código escrever." / "A IA digita rápido. Eu sei o que digitar." — [Software Nunca Está 'Pronto'](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/03/01/software-nunca-esta-pronto-4-projetos-a-vida-pos-deploy-e-por-que-one-shot-prompt-e-mito/)
- **Against one-shot:** "One-shot é pra demo. Iteração é pra produção." Specs can't anticipate production (his examples: Gmail clipping, Steam pt-BR date formats, MIME gaps), so features emerge from use — [same](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/03/01/software-nunca-esta-pronto-4-projetos-a-vida-pos-deploy-e-por-que-one-shot-prompt-e-mito/)
- **XP as steering:** TDD, CI, small releases, pair programming, refactoring. "Sem disciplina, IA produz código rápido que acumula dívida técnica mais rápido ainda." Every feature or bug fix ships with tests; he benchmarks (A/B) before implementing — [same](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/03/01/software-nunca-esta-pronto-4-projetos-a-vida-pos-deploy-e-por-que-one-shot-prompt-e-mito/)
- **Prompting as communication:** "Eu trato qualquer LLM exatamente como eu trataria um par humano numa sessão de pair programming." Each request states the goal, the intended approach (open to better proposals), what he does NOT want, and how success is validated. For a risky ROM task he required idempotent numbered scripts, SQLite progress state, zero deletions and a manual approval flag before moving files. "Depois que o contexto está firme, pergunte em vez de mandar." "Nove em cada dez vezes, o problema não é o modelo. É a pergunta." — [Por que as LLMs não te dão o resultado esperado / Por que eu prefiro Claude Code (2026-04-15)](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/04/15/como-falar-com-o-claude-code-efetivamente/)
- **Why Claude Code (Apr 2026):** He sees the models as roughly tied, so the harness decides: "E o harness do Claude Code, hoje, é simplesmente superior." He cites its visible to-do list ("Quando ele me diz 'terminei', eu sei que a lista inteira foi executada") and that an interruption doesn't kill the running task. Codex is his fallback — [same](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/04/15/como-falar-com-o-claude-code-efetivamente/)
- **Clean Code for agents:** Keep files under 500 lines (ideally 200–300) and functions small enough to fit in one tool call. Use greppable names, explicit types ("Código tipado dá gabarito imediato"), DRY as refactor safety, and headless one-command tests ("TDD virou obrigação técnica, não mais filosofia"). Also: early returns, error messages that carry context, standard formatter in pre-commit, structured JSON logs. For CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md: "nenhum LLM faz essas coisas por default", so they should be "curto, direto, imperativo, orientado a ações", with "cada linha gasta token de contexto — densidade importa". He provides a ~100-line, ~500-token template. "Código limpo nunca foi moda. Virou infraestrutura." — [Clean Code pra Agentes de IA (2026-04-20)](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/04/20/clean-code-para-agentes-de-ia/)
- **Tooling he built:** ai-jail (agent sandbox), ai-memory (project ledger plus a Markdown wiki as long-term memory, Karpathy-wiki style), ai-usagebar — [index](https://akitaonrails.com/); [Dicas e Toolkit (2026-05-24)](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/05/24/dicas-e-toolkit-de-ia-do-akita-ai-jail-ai-memory-ai-usagebar/)

**Positions against "process inflation" (Aug 2026)**
- "Harness, Loop Engineering, Graph Engineering são Bullshit." He calls these marketing taxonomies built on small real practices: "A inflação é o produto." In his benchmark a harness barely moves strong models (Grok 4.5/4.6 within about 1 point across harnesses): "harness bom é o que cobra menos e não atrapalha. O resto é o modelo." He calls a verified loop "testing and review renamed" — [Hot take (2026-08-18)](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/08/18/hot-take-harness-loop-engineering-graph-engineering-sao-bullshit/)
- **Against multi-agent:** "cada agente a mais multiplica os modos de falha." He says no multi-agent combination beat Opus alone in his April tests. He cites Anthropic's own multi-agent research post as saying such setups use about 15x more tokens and that coding is a poor fit because most coding tasks aren't truly parallelizable. He also says Anthropic attributed about 80% of the improvement to spending more tokens rather than to architecture — [Hot take](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/08/18/hot-take-harness-loop-engineering-graph-engineering-sao-bullshit/)
- **Against SDD:** "uma spec precisa o bastante pra gerar código correto já é um programa"; "prosa não compila"; "software emerge, não se planeja." He compares SDD to UML/MDA. His alternative is "Agile Vibe Coding": one strong agent in a simple loop, supervised by a human, using XP practices plus ai-memory — [Hot take](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/08/18/hot-take-harness-loop-engineering-graph-engineering-sao-bullshit/)

**Latest (Oct 2026)**
- "os LLMs chegaram, ou estão pertíssimo de chegar, no teto da arquitetura transformer." "A escolha de modelo virou quase indiferente." He says coding performance has plateaued since Opus 4.5 in his benchmarks. "LLM sozinho é um papagaio caro. LLM com agente é competente o suficiente pra trabalhar de verdade." "O modelo é commodity; a engenharia em volta é o diferencial." "Não é bala de prata. É um compilador novo pra uma disciplina velha." Agents need an oracle (tests, docs) or "the loop spins idle." Without test-first discipline and documented invariants, the result is slop — [LLMs + Agentes = competente o suficiente (2026-10-12)](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/10/12/llms-agentes-competente-o-suficiente/)
- He cites METR's finding that agent task horizons double about every 7 months, the Stanford AI Index (the gap between the top and 10th Chatbot Arena model fell from 12% to 5%), and prompt caching cutting repeated-context cost by up to 90% — [same](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/10/12/llms-agentes-competente-o-suficiente/)
- He also published "Você é um idiota se acredita nas propagandas enganosas da OpenAI, Anthropic, NVIDIA" (2026-09-09). He is skeptical of vendor claims — [index](https://akitaonrails.com/)

### Inferences
- Akita's "compilador novo pra uma disciplina velha" is very close to the TabNews post's abstraction-layer thesis. This probably explains the user's misattribution. Akita's version is much more operational and evidence-heavy (commits, tests, ratings).
- His "plateau / model is commodity" claim (Oct) sits awkwardly with his own METR citation (task horizons doubling about every 7 months) and his April claim that Claude Code's *harness* is superior while saying in August that harnesses barely matter. The reconciliation seems to be that harness UX (to-do list, interruptibility, caching) matters for the human, but not for benchmark scores.
- His productivity numbers are self-reported by one expert, on greenfield solo projects that he scores himself (none above 8/10). They are not comparable to team or legacy-code settings.

### Gaps
- I did not find Akita's total monetary spend (USD/BRL) on subscriptions or tokens. The marathon post gives only "não pague por token."
- I did not check his YouTube or X content directly. These notes rely on blog posts, which mirror his videos.
- I did not fetch the multi-agent research post itself, so his "80% from tokens" paraphrase of Anthropic is unverified here.

## Point-by-point alignment and divergence with Anthropic's recommendations

### Takeaway
Akita and the post agree with Anthropic on the fundamentals: the human steers, verification comes from tests and the environment, keep CLAUDE.md short and dense, work in small iterations, review, and keep things simple, especially avoiding unnecessary multi-agent setups. They diverge on three points. Akita dismisses spec-writing and harness engineering, while Anthropic recommends interview→SPEC.md and documents harness patterns for long-running agents. Akita relies on CLAUDE.md and human discipline, while Anthropic says rules that can't have exceptions belong in hooks. Akita downplays the role of subagents, while Anthropic uses them for clean context and unbiased review. On most points the evidence is anecdotal on both sides. Anthropic's has more systematic (internal) testing; Akita's is a single practitioner's well-documented log.

### Cited Findings
**Anthropic sources used**
- "Building effective agents" (19 Dec 2024): "we recommend finding the simplest solution possible, and only increasing complexity when needed". Frameworks "often create extra layers of abstraction that can obscure the underlying prompts". Agents should gain "'ground truth' from the environment at each step". They can "pause for human feedback at checkpoints". "Code solutions are verifiable through automated tests". Use stopping conditions — [Anthropic, Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)
- "Effective context engineering for AI agents" (29 Sep 2025): aim for "the smallest possible set of high-signal tokens". Context rot (recall falls as tokens grow). System prompts at the "right altitude". Just-in-time retrieval, compaction, structured note-taking, and sub-agents with "clean context windows" — [Anthropic, context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- "Effective harnesses for long-running agents" (26 Nov 2025): an initializer agent, a JSON feature list (200+ features, all initially failing), claude-progress.txt, "only one feature at a time", git commits used to revert, and end-to-end testing "as a human user would". Named failure modes are declaring victory too early and trying "to do too much at once" (one-shotting) — [Anthropic, long-running harnesses](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)
- Claude Code best practices (current docs): tell Claude what it can't see. Write specific prompts that say what "fixed" looks like. "Interview me until you could write the spec, then write it to SPEC.md", then build in a fresh session. Keep CLAUDE.md short ("every line in it takes a little of Claude's attention on every task") and include the exact test/typecheck/lint commands. On verification: "If it has nothing to run that tells it whether the work actually is done, 'looks done' is all it has to go on. In practice that makes you the test suite". Ask for *evidence*. Review with a fresh subagent via `/code-review`, and review against SPEC.md with "skip style". "If something has to happen every time, make it a hook, a deny rule, or a managed setting. CLAUDE.md is for guidelines". Keep the enforced set small. Subagents cost tokens and can be slower — [Claude Code docs, best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)

### Inferences
- Where they agree (tests as oracle, iteration, short context files, human accountability, simplicity), Akita reached these conclusions independently through practice. Anthropic reached them through internal experiments. Two independent sources converging is meaningful.
- The spec disagreement is mostly about terms. Akita attacks SDD as a heavyweight methodology. Anthropic's SPEC.md is a short definition of done that is also used for review. Akita's "goal / approach / what I don't want / how to validate" request is functionally a mini-spec.
- On harness, Akita measures single-task benchmark scores, where model quality dominates. Anthropic's harness post addresses multi-session, long-horizon work, where state handoff dominates. Each is probably right in its own regime. Akita's own ai-memory and progress tracking are harness engineering in practice.
- Anthropic recommends deterministic enforcement (hooks, Stop-hook typecheck, deny rules) and automated fresh-context review. These are the clearest additions to Akita's human-discipline-centric approach and would reduce the "slop" he says vibe coding always produces.

### Gaps
- Anthropic does not publish controlled productivity studies in these posts. Its recommendations come from internal practice and are not independently replicated.
- I found no head-to-head comparison of "Akita-style XP steering" versus "Anthropic-style spec + hooks + automated review".

## Empirical evidence supporting or contradicting the claims

### Takeaway
The best controlled evidence (METR RCTs) contradicts large productivity claims for experienced developers working on mature codebases, at least for early 2025 (19% slower). METR's late-2025/2026 follow-up points toward speedups but is inconclusive and biased by selection, and METR itself calls it an unreliable signal. Akita's 5–10x figures are self-reported, greenfield, single-developer and unblinded, with self-rated quality of 1–8/10. They support "fast prototyping" more than "5–10x on production software." Evidence for the *practices* (tests as oracle, incremental work) is stronger and comes from both Anthropic's experiments and Akita's logs.

### Cited Findings
- METR early-2025 RCT: AI-assisted tasks took **19% longer** (95% CI +2% to +39%) for experienced open-source developers — [METR, 2026-02-24 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)
- The same 2025 study is widely reported as showing developers *believed* AI made them about 20% faster. This was not confirmed on the METR update page; it comes from secondary coverage — [ScienceBlog summary](https://scienceblog.com/t-a-randomized-trial-by-metr-found-that-experienced-developers-completed-real-coding-tasks-19-slower-when-allowed-to-use-ai-tools-yet-afterwards-they-estimated-on-average-that-ai-had-made-them-20-fast/)
- METR follow-up (started Aug 2025): 57 developers, 143 repos, 800+ tasks. Returning developers' estimated time change was **-18%** (CI -38% to +9%); new developers' was **-4%** (CI -15% to +9%). METR calls this "some evidence for speedup", but both intervals include zero — [METR update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)
- METR calls the new data an "unreliable signal". Many developers refused to participate without AI, and 30–50% withheld tasks they didn't want to do without AI, so the estimate is likely a lower bound. METR believes developers were likely more sped up in early 2026 than in early 2025, but the data is "very weak evidence", and it is changing the study design — [METR update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)
- Akita cites METR's finding that the task length agents complete at 50% reliability doubles about every 7 months. This supports his claim that agents are now "competent enough" — [Akita Oct 2026](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/10/12/llms-agentes-competente-o-suficiente/)
- Anthropic's harness experiments: without incremental feature lists and E2E testing, Claude declared victory early and tried to one-shot. Adding these practices improved outcomes (described qualitatively). This supports Akita's anti-one-shot and TDD claims — [Anthropic harness post](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)
- Akita's own outcome data cuts against hype: no project above 8/10, several at 1–2/10, many abandoned; vibe coding "always produces slop" — [Akita May 2026](https://akitaonrails.com/2026/05/14/terminando-maratona-ia-sucesso-ou-fracasso/)

### Inferences
- The post's "ganhos absurdos de produtividade" and Akita's 5–10x are not supported by controlled studies. The best RCT evidence ranges from a slowdown (2025) to an inconclusive, possibly sizeable speedup (2026, selection-biased). Speedups are most plausible on greenfield, solo or prototype work (Akita's setting) and least established on mature team codebases (METR's setting).
- Akita's observation that METR-type perception gaps exist is consistent with his "AI reflects and accelerates who you are": gains depend on the user's expertise and discipline. METR's selection problems (developers refusing to work without AI) are indirect evidence that practitioners perceive substantial value.

### Gaps
- I found no independent RCT of Claude Code specifically, or of agentic (vs. autocomplete-era) tools on production codebases, that gives a clean 2026 estimate.
- No quality-adjusted productivity data (defects, maintenance cost) exists for Akita's projects beyond his self-ratings.
- I opened only METR's primary page. The other METR-related search results were secondary blogs with partly conflicting details and were not used.
